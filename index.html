Our Data

The report follows the same order as our pdf presentation file: data sources first, then wealth, corruption, health and development, economic growth, tourism, labour markets, purchasing power, and synthesis.

The first group of files shown in the screenshot (corruption.csv, cost_of_living.csv, richest_countries.csv, tourism.csv, and unemployment.csv) comes from World Economic Data. The other two files (Economic Indicators And Inflation.csv and WorldBank.xlsx) come from Global Economic & Human Development Data.

The libraries that were used are tidyverse, ggplot2, ggrepel (clean plot labels), scales (axis and dollar formatting), readxl, and knitr (better looking tables).

clean <- function(df) {
  df %>% drop_na() %>% mutate(across(where(is.character), trimws))
}

corruption   <- clean(read.csv("data/corruption.csv"))
cost_living  <- clean(read.csv("data/cost_of_living.csv"))
richest      <- clean(read.csv("data/richest_countries.csv"))
tourism      <- clean(read.csv("data/tourism.csv"))
unemployment <- clean(read.csv("data/unemployment.csv"))
ec           <- read.csv("data/Economic Indicators And Inflation.csv")
bank         <- read_xlsx("data/WorldBank.xlsx")

world <- corruption %>%
  full_join(cost_living,  by = "country") %>%
  full_join(richest,      by = "country") %>%
  full_join(tourism,      by = "country") %>%
  full_join(unemployment, by = "country") %>%
  classify_country() %>%
  mutate(
    income_tier = case_when(
      annual_income >= 50000 ~ "High Income (>= $50k)",
      annual_income >= 15000 ~ "Upper-Middle ($15k-50k)",
      annual_income >= 5000  ~ "Lower-Middle ($5k-15k)",
      !is.na(annual_income)  ~ "Low Income (< $5k)"
    ),
    income_tier = factor(income_tier, levels = c(
      "High Income (>= $50k)", "Upper-Middle ($15k-50k)",
      "Lower-Middle ($5k-15k)", "Low Income (< $5k)"
    )),
    value_ratio = purchasing_power_index / cost_index,
    col_quadrant = case_when(
      cost_index >= 100 & purchasing_power_index >= 70 ~ "Expensive & High Power",
      cost_index >= 100 & purchasing_power_index < 70  ~ "Expensive & Low Power",
      cost_index <  100 & purchasing_power_index >= 50 ~ "Affordable & High Power",
      !is.na(cost_index)                               ~ "Affordable & Low Power"
    ),
    unemp_rank = rank(desc(unemployment_rate), na.last = "keep"),
    unemp_z    = round(
      (unemployment_rate - mean(unemployment_rate, na.rm = TRUE)) /
        sd(unemployment_rate, na.rm = TRUE), 2)
  )

Results and key takeaways

Accross the datasets, countries that are weak in one dimension typically are also weak in others. It has a logical path of: low income goes towards weak institutions, which then result in lower purchasing power and growth rate, which then can end in health problems. Despite the the presence of strong correlation and connection between this factors this is not a destiny - outliers prove it. Estonia is a good example of it, while at the same time resource-rich country countries prove that income does not mean institutional quality for a fact.
